## Borgahällan Northeast Face

My car was broken, and I was at home looking at the forecast for the next few days. Cold and clear weather, good for winter climbing. I wanted to get out, and eventually I took the train to Östersund and (after some misfortunes) managed to rent a car for a couple of days.

The day after I left home at 03:30 and drove to Borgafjäll. I arrived at the parking by the dam at 07:45 with the car thermometer showing -25. Cold and crisp, but considering that only 20 minutes earlier it had been down to -31, it still felt like a win.

I quickly changed into winter climbing gear and started to walk across the dam on a snowmobile track toward Borgahällan's impressive northeast face. It wasn't long before the sun's first rays hit the upper part of the mountain and instantly turned it red. I wanted to climb Rännan, so once I'd made it across the dam I headed straight up the slope to the steep gully (ränna, in Swedish, hence the route name) that makes up the first half of that route.

At 09:45 I was standing right below the wall, looking up the steep gully that I was about to climb, a trickle of spindrift coming down its center. I thought about turning back, but then for some reason decided to keep going. At first the snow was soft enough for step-kicking, but soon patches of ice appeared under the thin snow cover, and I found myself swinging my ice tools and trying to duck-walk to save my calves from getting pumped.

Then the ice got steeper, and after a pitch of rotten WI3 climbing I made it to a small snow field right below the near-vertical ice chimney that I had heard was the crux of the route. The chimney actually looked like great fun from a distance, but once I started climbing it I found the same rotten ice that I had found down below, and I had to work a bit to find good tool and crampon placements. I would probably grade it about WI3+ in the conditions I found it in.

Once past the chimney another pitch of WI3 followed, after which I traversed out left onto an exposed snow ramp before continuing up and left through rock bands and steep snow fields. This second, mixed part of the route turned out to be more difficult that I had expected, and I found myself right on the edge of my comfort zone on several occasions. After what seemed like a really long time, I finally pulled myself over the edge of the wall and onto the wide summit plateau at 12:20.

After having sat down for a few minutes to catch my breath I walked over to the summit cairn, located about a hundred meters north of where I topped out. Breathtaking views, as expected. But the days are short in January, and it was getting noticeably colder (or was I just sweaty and tired after the climb?), so I didn't waste any time and immediately started the descent.

At first the snow was hard and easy to walk on. But once I made it down to the birch forest it quickly became soft and deep and every step became a struggle as a sunk to the hip over and over again. At 14:30 I was back at the car, cold and tired but happy to have made it up and down the mountain in one piece after a climb that had become quite a bit more exciting than I had initially expected.
